# Outcomes evidence framework

## 1. Purpose

Routine court statistics mainly describe demand, process and formal outputs. They rarely establish what happens to children and families after proceedings. The outcomes evidence catalogue is therefore a separate but linked product rather than an extra column added to caseflow data.

## 2. Outcome domains

### Administrative outcomes

- appeal and reversal;
- enforcement and non-compliance;
- repeat application or return to court;
- subsequent protection proceedings;
- placement changes, reunification or adoption events where lawfully reported;
- order implementation and service completion.

### User and procedural-justice outcomes

- understanding and ability to participate;
- perceived fairness and voice;
- respect, trust and satisfaction;
- accessibility and burden;
- legal representation and support experience;
- safety during process.

### Child and family outcomes

- safety and recurrence of harm;
- stability of care, residence or contact arrangements;
- wellbeing and mental/physical health;
- education and social participation;
- family functioning and conflict;
- economic security;
- durable resolution and absence of harmful repeat litigation.

An order type is a court output, not itself proof of safety, wellbeing or stability.

## 3. Evidence types

Catalogue separately:

- official administrative reporting;
- court-user surveys;
- legal-needs/population surveys;
- programme/service evaluations;
- longitudinal cohort studies;
- linked administrative-data studies;
- qualitative and mixed-methods research;
- systematic reviews and evidence syntheses;
- validated outcome instruments and measurement frameworks.

## 4. Minimum metadata

Each evidence record should identify:

- jurisdiction and responsible institutions;
- publication and data-collection dates;
- matter type and procedural population;
- inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- study design and comparator where relevant;
- sample size, response/attrition and representativeness;
- outcome definition, instrument and reporter;
- follow-up period and timing relative to proceedings;
- administrative data sources/linkage method where applicable;
- statistical adjustment and uncertainty;
- equity strata and disclosure controls;
- source, exact provenance, rights and review status;
- limitations, conflicts and funding;
- quality appraisal and comparability notes.

## 5. Quality appraisal

The court-source A–E grade does not adequately appraise outcome studies. v1 should retain a separate appraisal appropriate to design, considering:

- population and selection bias;
- measurement validity and consistency;
- response/attrition and missing data;
- temporal ordering and follow-up;
- confounding and causal identification where claims are made;
- linkage quality and outcome ascertainment;
- reporting completeness and reproducibility;
- transferability across legal and service contexts;
- conflicts and independence.

The catalogue may include lower-certainty evidence, but the appraisal and use restriction must be visible.

## 6. Comparability

Do not pool outcome estimates merely because labels match. Check:

- population and matter type;
- order/proceeding stage;
- reporter and instrument;
- follow-up window;
- denominator and attrition;
- service context and legal threshold;
- whether the measure is prevalence, change, recurrence or time-to-event;
- whether analysis is descriptive, adjusted association or causal estimate.

International synthesis may begin as an evidence map rather than a meta-analysis.

## 7. Linked-data boundary

Any person-level linkage requires a separate secure project with:

- lawful basis and custodian approvals;
- ethics and child-rights review;
- data minimisation and purpose limitation;
- secure environment and access controls;
- linkage-quality assessment;
- disclosure review and safe outputs;
- community/lived-experience governance;
- rules preventing individual or family identification.

Only study metadata and safe aggregate findings enter the public v1 repository.

## 8. v1.0 outcome product

v1.0 should deliver:

- a structured outcomes evidence register;
- transparent inclusion/search methods;
- study/design appraisal fields;
- links to source evidence;
- jurisdiction, matter and outcome-domain coverage maps;
- an evidence-gap report;
- clear separation from routine court-performance comparisons.

It need not deliver globally harmonised causal outcome estimates. That is a later research programme requiring sufficiently comparable evidence.
