# Governance framework

## Bodies

### Steering group

Sets strategic priorities, approves the annual work programme and protects independence. It should include judicial administration, family-law practice, child rights, family violence expertise, quantitative methods, comparative law, economics, open data and lived experience.

### Methods and standards group

Owns the matter taxonomy, indicator dictionary, quality grades, comparability tiers, suppression rules and methods revisions. Minutes and decisions should be public.

### Data operations group

Maintains acquisition, extraction, validation, releases, corrections and preservation.

### Jurisdiction network

Local correspondents verify institutional structures, terminology and source interpretation. Verification is recorded; disagreement is preserved in notes rather than silently resolved.

## Decision rules

- Method changes require a versioned proposal, evidence, impact assessment and recorded decision.
- Previously released values are not overwritten; corrections generate a new release and changelog entry.
- Funders and participating courts may comment on accuracy but do not control conclusions.
- Contributors declare financial, professional and institutional conflicts.
- Lived-experience contributors are remunerated and supported to participate safely.

## Publication safeguards

- Aggregate data only in the public repository.
- Minimum cell-size and dominance rules where demographic breakdowns could identify families.
- No publication of protected judgments, sealed material or data obtained in breach of access conditions.
- Context notes accompany comparisons involving statutory targets, case-mix or procedural reforms.
- The initial project does not issue a composite ranking of jurisdictions.
