# Benefits, harms and responsible-use evaluation

## 1. Purpose

The project may improve transparency and learning, but international justice comparisons can also mislead, stigmatise or create gaming incentives. Evaluation therefore covers not only whether people use the product, but whether use is valid, equitable and safe.

## 2. Intended benefits

- make public reporting discoverable across jurisdictions;
- expose where data are absent, inaccessible or poorly defined;
- improve court and justice-agency performance reporting;
- support defensible comparison of compatible measures;
- reduce duplicate source discovery and extraction work;
- improve transparency about waiting, backlog, access and process;
- connect routine court statistics with a broader outcomes evidence base;
- help researchers and administrators identify data and evaluation gaps;
- strengthen multilingual and regional participation in justice-data standards.

## 3. Foreseeable harms

- simplistic league tables or media rankings;
- treating faster processing as inherently better for children/families;
- ignoring legal thresholds, case mix, resources or reporting capacity;
- penalising jurisdictions that report more transparently;
- interpreting missing data as zero activity or poor performance;
- conflating court outputs with safety/wellbeing outcomes;
- exposing sensitive small groups through disaggregation;
- reputational or funding consequences from incorrect extraction;
- centralising English-language interpretations over local legal meaning;
- creating incentives to change reporting definitions rather than practice;
- concentrating influence in well-resourced institutions or regions.

## 4. Safeguards to evaluate

- visible comparability tiers and definitions;
- absence of a composite v1 ranking;
- preserved original-language wording;
- domain-specific missingness reasons;
- accessible source/provenance links;
- contextual jurisdiction profiles;
- correction response and transparent revision history;
- disclosure and contextual-harm controls;
- balanced regional and lived-experience governance;
- constraints preventing Tier 3/4 direct comparison;
- responsible-use guidance and media/research briefings.

## 5. Evaluation questions

### Usefulness

- Can intended users find the relevant jurisdiction/source/indicator?
- Can they determine whether a comparison is permitted and why?
- Does the project reduce time spent locating and interpreting sources?
- Do court/statistics agencies use the framework to improve reporting?

### Validity

- Are published values and provenance accurate?
- Are users interpreting clocks, units and cohorts correctly?
- Are corrections and source revisions handled transparently?
- Does the gold cohort remain stable under independent review?

### Equity and legitimacy

- Which regions, languages and legal traditions shape methods and releases?
- Are correspondents and lived-experience contributors materially influential?
- Are lower-capacity jurisdictions disproportionately classified as “no data” because search resources are weaker?
- Are core products accessible under low bandwidth and without proprietary tools?

### Harms and misuse

- Are outputs being converted into unsupported rankings or causal claims?
- Have any releases created disclosure, safety or reputational harm?
- Are product constraints and context notes reducing misinterpretation?
- Do correction and takedown processes work when harm is reported?

## 6. Measures

Track at least:

- search/task success in user testing;
- definition/comparability comprehension;
- source-to-release error and correction rate;
- proportion of external uses that preserve source/limitations in a sampled review;
- reported misuse or misleading ranking incidents;
- regional/language representation in contributors and reviewers;
- negative-finding rates by region/language and re-review results;
- accessibility defects and low-bandwidth completion;
- correction acknowledgement/disposition performance;
- stakeholder trust and perceived fairness of the project’s process.

Metrics should be interpreted qualitatively; high download counts are not evidence of responsible impact.

## 7. Evaluation cadence

- formative testing during pilot and public beta;
- pre-release harms review for v1.0;
- 90-day post-launch review;
- annual benefits/harms report during 1.x;
- immediate review after a P0 privacy, integrity or major misuse incident;
- methods/product changes in response to findings through public decision records.
