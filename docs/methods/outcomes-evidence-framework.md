# Outcomes evidence framework

## Purpose

The project must distinguish a court’s administrative performance from what happens to children, families, and court users. The outcomes evidence catalogue records what evidence exists and what it can support; it does not convert weak evidence into a comparative outcome score.

## Evidence classes

- routine administrative outcome reporting;
- court-user surveys;
- population legal-needs surveys;
- programme or procedural evaluations;
- prospective or retrospective cohorts;
- linked administrative studies;
- qualitative and mixed-methods research;
- systematic reviews and evidence syntheses.

## Outcome domains

### Access and participation

Ability to obtain help, understand procedure, participate, afford representation, use interpreters or adjustments, and navigate digital or physical services.

### Procedural justice and experience

Voice, neutrality, respect, trust, comprehension, perceived fairness, satisfaction, and safety during proceedings.

### Safety and protection

Repeat violence, breach, recurrence, protective-order enforcement, child-protection recurrence, and perceived safety. Definitions and observation windows are essential.

### Stability and continuity

Placement stability, parenting-arrangement stability, housing or care continuity, reunification, and repeat applications.

### Compliance and subsequent justice contact

Order compliance, maintenance payment, enforcement, appeal, reapplication, return to court, and subsequent related proceedings.

### Wellbeing and family functioning

Validated or clearly described measures of child, parent, caregiver, and family wellbeing and functioning.

### Equity

Differences in access, process, outcomes, and experience by geography and population characteristics where lawful, ethical, meaningful, and non-disclosive.

## Minimum evidence record

Each evidence item should identify:

- jurisdiction and setting;
- population and matter type;
- study period and publication date;
- design, comparator, sample, and data sources;
- outcome domains and exact measures;
- follow-up duration;
- peer-review or official status;
- risk-of-bias or quality assessment where appropriate;
- effect estimates and reusable aggregate data availability;
- exact source/provenance and review status;
- limitations affecting interpretation or transportability.

## Interpretation rules

- Descriptive associations are not causal effects.
- Court throughput and timeliness are not proxies for wellbeing or safety.
- Satisfaction alone is not proof of legal fairness or substantive outcome quality.
- Repeat court contact can indicate unresolved conflict, changed circumstances, improved access, or enforcement need; interpretation requires context.
- Absence of published evidence is not evidence of absence of an outcome.
- Cross-jurisdiction synthesis requires compatible populations, matter types, interventions/exposures, outcomes, and designs.

## v1 boundary

Version 1.0 aims to publish a high-quality evidence catalogue and evidence-gap map. Formal meta-analysis, common outcome instruments, or person-level linkage partnerships are separate governed work unless they independently meet v1 release standards.
