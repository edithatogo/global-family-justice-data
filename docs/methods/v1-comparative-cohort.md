# v1 comparative cohort rule

## 1. Purpose

The global source census includes every in-scope jurisdiction. The v1 comparative cohort includes only series that can support a specified comparison. This prevents broad geographic ambition from weakening analytical validity.

## 2. Eligibility

A series is eligible for gold comparative use only when:

- source authority and provenance meet the approved threshold;
- matter type and count unit are clear;
- statistic, unit, period and cohort basis are explicit;
- duration clock start/end and treatment of inactive time are known where relevant;
- inclusion/exclusion rules are sufficiently documented;
- source/series-level second review is complete;
- automated validation passes;
- comparability tier is 1 or 2 for the proposed output;
- disclosure and rights checks pass;
- series breaks are represented rather than hidden.

## 3. Cohort construction

The cohort manifest records:

- release and analytical purpose;
- indicator/matter combination;
- eligible series and periods;
- reasons for inclusion and exclusion;
- transformations/restrictions for Tier 2;
- quality and review status;
- context variables required for interpretation;
- sensitivity or alternative cohort analyses.

## 4. Publication rules

- Tier 1 may be directly compared with definitions visible.
- Tier 2 must show the restriction/transformation and avoid stronger claims.
- Tier 3 may appear in profiles or side-by-side descriptive tables but not ranked.
- Tier 4 remains in the source/local-analysis layer only.
- A missing value must expose the reason: not reported, not searched, inaccessible, not applicable, failed quality or not comparable.

## 5. Proposed v1 target

As a target rather than a licence to lower standards, v1 should seek gold data from at least 30 jurisdictions spanning all programme regions. The binding requirement is method compliance; a smaller valid cohort is preferable to a larger misleading one.
